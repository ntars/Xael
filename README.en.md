# Xael · 玄玖灵犀

> *The mystery of mysteries — the gate of all wonders.* (Laozi, Daodejing)
>
> Xuanjiu Lingxi · Xael.

**Xael** (玄玖灵犀) is a local, offline library of traditional Chinese mantic literature (术数), also serving as a toolkit for collating, proofreading and digitally computing that corpus. It computes the calendar, Four Pillars, Zi Wei Dou Shu, Qi Men Dun Jia, Da Liu Ren, Liu Yao, Mei Hua Yi Shu, Xuan Kong Fei Xing and the almanac **from first principles, on your device** — no lookup tables, no network, no third-party divination API.

## 1. Name and Positioning

**Name.** 「玄玖」draws on 「玄之又玄」("the mystery of mysteries") and echoes the 玖 of the studio name 星玖; 「灵犀」refers to the rhinoceros horn said to join kindred minds — here, results that stay faithful to the original intent of the source texts.

**One-line positioning.** An offline corpus that computes Chinese mantic arts from the calendar foundation up to the chart conclusion, with reproducible results, auditable conventions and readable code.

**Three boundaries.**

1. **A textual tool, not a fortune-telling service.** Every conclusion carries its basis (which book, which chapter, under what condition). Items without a basis are not generated.
2. **A local program, not an online API.** No network, no third-party divination API, no upload of birth data. Working offline is a hard requirement.
3. **A collatable edition, not an unquestionable black box.** Every school choice lives in `docs/CALIBER-NOTES.md`; switching schools means changing one table.

## 2. Overview

### 2.1 Background

Divination software sits on two unsatisfying poles: closed commercial charting sites (opaque algorithms, undocumented conventions, no offline use) and scattered open scripts (single-purpose, no shared calendar base, impossible to audit). Anyone who wants to read, verify and modify seriously has almost no ground to stand on.

### 2.2 Problems addressed

1. **Calendar.** The Chinese lunisolar calendar needs true new moons and true solar terms. Table-driven code fails on boundary days — exactly where accuracy matters most (leap-month attribution, month change at the solar term). Any table-based implementation also fails around 1900 and in future years.
2. **Convention.** Zi Wei four transformations, Qi Men Dun Jia's *dingyuan* rule and Liu Ren's *fayong* chain each have competing schools; mixing them silently produces wrong charts. Without a declared convention, users cannot tell which school a result belongs to.
3. **Verifiability.** A result without a stated basis cannot be checked. A conclusion that cannot be traced to source text is a claim, not a testable inference.

### 2.3 Core approach

Strict layering:

| Layer | Location | Responsibility | Explicitly not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calendar | `src/core/calendar/` | Solar longitude, true new moons and solar-term instants from astronomical series in real time | No mantic rules |
| Rules | `src/core/four-pillars/`, `purple-star/`, `mystic-gates/`, etc. | Consume only normalized sexagenary and term data | Never computes the calendar |
| Shells | `miniprogram/`, site root, `uni-app` | Interaction and rendering | No algorithms |

Pipeline: Gregorian → Julian Day → solar longitude (truncated VSOP87 series + nutation / aberration / ΔT) → true new moon (the Shou Xing precision table `SHUO_KB` before 1985, series after) → month from the new-moon day → leap month inserted between two winter solstices when a month contains no principal term.

### 2.4 Use cases

- Textual collation and manual chart verification by researchers;
- Developer integration (birth data → Four Pillars, term reminders, almanac data);
- Teaching traditional calendrics (every intermediate value is inspectable);
- Products needing a transparent self-built core.

### 2.5 Non-goals

- No accuracy claims about prediction: results follow a stated convention; they are not assertions about the future.
- No consultation service: no readings, no master endorsement.
- No cloud account: no user system, no history sync.
- No items lacking textual basis: omission over invention.

## 4. Algorithm Reference

This chapter introduces the eight categories and 22 functions one by one. Each entry gives five things: **which chapter of which text it rests on**, **how the algorithm computes**, **what results it produces**, **how school disagreements are handled**, and **which evidence tier it sits in**.

The three tiers come first; later entries only cite the tier.

| Tier | Meaning | How it is enforced |
|---|---|---|
| **A Direct textual proof** | The rule and the reading can both be found in the base text shipped in `refs-texts/` | Enforced by `npm run check:cites` (chapter titles traceable) and `npm run check:readings` (every clause of a reading traceable) |
| **B Structurally self-consistent** | The text gives the structural relation, but the text itself says the absolute starting point "cannot be investigated" | Output states "structurally self-consistent, anchor unverifiable" |
| **C Outside corpus / conventional** | Absent from the 20 texts in the repository; taken from modern compilations or common mnemonics | Output and documentation are marked "outside corpus", "modern compilation" or "unverified" |

**The 20 base texts** in `refs-texts/` (all public domain): *Zhou Yi*, *Yuan Hai Zi Ping*, *Di Tian Sui*, *Zi Ping Zhen Quan*, *San Ming Tong Hui*, *Qiong Tong Bao Jian*, *Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu* (two editions), *Qi Men Dun Jia Tong Zong*, *Qi Men Bao Jian*, *Dun Jia Yan Yi*, *Liu Ren Da Quan*, *Da Liu Ren Zhi Nan*, *Ren Gui*, *Bi Fa Fu*, *Bu Shi Zheng Zong*, *Zeng Shan Bu Yi*, *Huang Jin Ce*, *Mei Hua Yi Shu*, *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, *Yu Ding Xing Li Kao Yuan*, *Yu Xia Ji*.

### 4.1 The calendar foundation: the ground everything stands on

The calendar is not one function but the shared premise of all 22. If it is wrong, every chart above it is wrong.

#### 4.1.1 Basis

| Content | Source |
|---|---|
| Astronomical series and constants | Outside corpus: Jean Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms*, 2nd ed. (algorithmic ideas are not copyrightable; the implementation here is independent) |
| True new moon and true solar term method | Outside corpus: *Shi Xian Li* |
| Sexagenary day count | Julian Day mod 60 (continuous day count, no breakpoints) |

#### 4.1.2 Algorithm

Five files, layer by layer, **using no almanac table at all**:

```
julian.js       Gregorian ↔ Julian Day (Fliegel–Van Flandern integer formula), day number, weekday
     ↓
astronomy.js    Apparent solar longitude (truncated VSOP87 series)
                True new moon instants (JDE chapter series)
                ΔT (Espenak–Meeus polynomials)
                Dynamical time ↔ Beijing time conversion
     ↓
solar-terms.js  Solves "apparent solar longitude = solar term degree" by "circle number + phase", to the second
     ↓
lunisolar.js    Month from the new-moon day
                "13 new moons between two winter solstices → the first month without a principal term is leap; 12 → no leap"
     ↓
sexagenary.js   Sexagenary (Julian Day mod 60)
                Month changes at solar terms, year at spring begins, hour stem by the five-rat escape
```

**Why recompute instead of looking up**: table-driven code is always wrong on boundary days, and boundary days are exactly where accuracy matters most — leap-month attribution and month change both land on those instants. Tables have a more fundamental problem too: they fail outright outside their coverage, and cannot answer "why is this the first day of a leap month".

**Four hard sexagenary rules** (hard-coded, not configurable):

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Year boundary | The instant of spring begins (立春) |
| Month boundary | The solar term instant |
| Day boundary | Changed at the start of the zi hour (23:00) |
| Zodiac animal | Follows the year pillar's branch |
| Hour stem | Computed live by the five-rat escape: `hour stem = (day stem index×2 + hour branch index) % 10` |

#### 4.1.3 What it produces

| Capability | Precision / coverage |
|---|---|
| Gregorian ↔ Julian Day | Integer arithmetic, no accumulated error |
| Solar term instants | To the second; 3,576 terms from 1901–2049 compared: mean deviation 0 s, median 0 s, p99 = 1 s, maximum 60 s, all dates identical |
| Lunar month and day, leap months | All 73,049 days from 1901–2100, zero difference against the reference implementation |
| Sexagenary four pillars | Year, month, day, hour, with boundary handling for day and year changes |
| Zodiac, weekday, festivals, statutory holidays | Zodiac follows the year pillar; festivals per outside-corpus seasonal records and current statutory lists |

#### 4.1.4 One deliberately preserved boundary convention

**When the solar term instant equals the birth instant, it is treated as "not yet crossed".**

Reason: the reference implementation rounds the term instant to the minute in its display layer, and the second-level residual is generally positive, so "born exactly at that minute" is in fact still before the term. This project pads the seconds field before comparing strings, avoiding the false ordering of `'22:00:0' < '22:00:00'`.

**Late zi hour and the year boundary**: the year and month pillars judge spring begins and the term crossing from the *original birth instant* (for example, at 23:00 on 1901-2-3 the day pillar has already advanced to 2-4, but spring begins has not arrived, so the year pillar is still the previous year's geng-zi); the day pillar still changes at 23:00; the hour stem is computed by the five-rat escape from the post-change day stem.

#### 4.1.5 Tier

**A (astronomical part)**. Astronomical series are pure mathematics and independently verifiable; all 3,576 solar terms match.

### 4.2 Calendar and almanac

#### 4.2.1 Daily almanac

**Basis**

| Content | Source |
|---|---|
| Principles, day deities, yellow-black path | *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, chapters 4–9 (principles), chapter 7 (yellow and black paths) quoting outside-corpus *Shen Shu Jing* |
| 28 mansions paired with days | *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, chapter 1, quoting outside-corpus *Su Yao Jing* |
| Twelve day officers (建除) | *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, "Twelve Day Officers" |
| Wealth and fortune deity directions | *Yu Xia Ji*, "Song of the Wealth Deity's Direction", "Song of the Fortune Deity's Direction" |
| 28-mansion auspiciousness | *Yu Xia Ji*, "Song of the 28 Mansions' Daily Fortune" |
| Joy deity moods | *Yu Xia Ji*, "Song of the Joy Deity's Moods" |
| Daily auspicious hours | *Yu Xia Ji*, "Song of Auspicious Hours by Day" |
| Three eight-mansion songs | *Yu Xia Ji*, "Song of the Nine Stars' Yearly Portents", "Song of Offspring", "Song of Chamber Fortunes" |
| Four废 (four废 days) | *Xie Ji*, "Four废" |
| Yang Gong taboo, visit-sick taboo, great bright days | *Yu Xia Ji* |
| Nobles (official-school) | *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, prefatory chapter quoting *Li Hai Ji*; the *Siku* abstract of *Liu Ren Da Quan* corrects the popular mnemonic accordingly |

**Algorithm**

The twelve day officers are determined by the **difference between the day branch and the month-establishment branch**, not by day number:

```
index = (day branch − month branch) mod 12
sequence = 建 除 满 平 定 执 破 危 成 收 开 闭
```

Per the original wording "start 建 from the month establishment…… if the first month establishes yin, then the yin day starts 建 and proceeds through the twelve branches". The original implementation wrongly derived the branch by "day number mod 12" and has been corrected.

Yellow and black path deities: blue dragon, bright hall, golden casket, heavenly virtue, jade hall and life mandate are the yellow path; the other six are the black path. All 144 cells are recomputed against the original — "blue dragon starts at zi in the first month, golden casket at chen, life mandate at xu (forward through the six yang branches); bright hall starts at chou in the first month, heavenly virtue at si, jade hall at wei (forward through the six yin branches)" — with zero mismatches and a guarding assertion.

**Recomputed auspicious and baleful deities**: **43 deities** are derived item by item from the "day deities taking fixed stems and branches" section of *Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, chapter 9, including heavenly grace, crowing, crowing response, five harmony, removal deity, release deity, eight专, water-touching dragon, doubled day and others. **Six deliberately not derived**: precious day, righteous day, controlling day, dedicated day, punitive day and no-salary — the compendium does fix their stems and branches, but the two almanac columns "auspicious deities to approach" and "baleful deities to avoid" never list them (zero occurrences across all 336 days of 2024), so deriving them would only lower the match rate and mislead readers.

| Deities | `值星` (value star), `天神` (day deity), `天神类` (class), `天神吉凶` (fortune), `天神断语` (reading), `天神别名` (alias) |
| 28 mansions | `二十八宿` |
| Directions | `方位` (joy, wealth, fortune, yang noble, yin noble deities) |
| Taboos | `彭祖百忌`, `胎神` (foetal deity), `冲煞` (clash), `特别忌` |
| Activities | `宜` (suitable), `忌` (unsuitable), `吉神宜趋`, `凶煞宜忌`, `历例吉神`, `历例凶煞` |
| Festivals | `节日`, `法定假期`, `特别吉` |

**School disagreements and self-limits**

| Disagreement | How this project handles it |
|---|---|
| Whether the joy deity is a day or hour matter | *Xie Ji*, chapter 9 ("Correcting Errors") argues it belongs to the hour school and that a daily table is wrong in every case; this layer keeps a day-stem table because the almanac needs one row, and states the disagreement |
| Whether the 28 mansions can be derived | *Xie Ji* explicitly denies it ("to fit an uneven heaven to fixed mansions is in no way a workable principle"); this layer uses a modern almanac anchor (Thursday = horn mansion) and **does not claim textual proof** |
| Noble deity derivation | The old table was the popular mnemonic and had two wrong entries for the geng stem; replaced with the official-school stem-combination derivation |
| Daily activities | The popular *Yu Xia Ji* edition has only categorised auspicious days and monthly taboos, no full "sixty sexagenary days × daily activities" table; the 3,600 cells are internally consistent, but all 300 month-breach cells still list activities as suitable, quantified in the output |
| Foetal deity | The popular edition has only "the deity's location by day", no sixty-day foetal table; marked unverified |

### 4.3 Four Pillars (four functions)

Four Pillars takes the four sexagenary pairs of the birth instant as input and reasons about five-element relations. All four functions share one chart output and each adds a layer on top.

#### 4.3.1 Four Pillars chart

**Basis**

| Content | Source |
|---|---|

### 4.8 Feng Shui (two functions)

#### 4.8.1 Life gua (eight mansions)

**Basis**: *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, chapter 2, "Great Wandering Year Hexagram Changes" (hexagram-flipping method); *Yu Xia Ji*, "Song of the Great Wandering Year" and "Nine Stars' Yin-Yang and Fortune" (placement order and grades); *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, chapter 34, "Men's and Women's Nine Palaces" quoting outside-corpus *San Yuan Jing*.

**Algorithm**

Life gua: add the last two digits of the birth year to a single digit → subtract from 11 for males, add 4 for females; a result of 5 becomes gen-8 for males and kun-2 for females; 10 becomes 9 (li) for males and 1 (kan) for females.

The eight wandering-year stars follow the hexagram-flipping method: starting from the home palace's three lines, flipping each position in the sequence "top, middle, bottom, middle, top, middle, bottom, middle" yields in order:

```
Life (greedy wolf, wood) → Five Ghosts (chaste, fire) → Longevity (martial, metal) → Six Killings (literary, water)
→ Mishap (emolument, earth) → Heavenly Doctor (giant gate, earth) → Severed Life (broken army, metal) → Fixed Position (assistant, wood)
```

Original: "Qian's top line changes to dui as greedy wolf, dui's middle line changes to zhen as five ghosts, zhen's bottom line changes to kun as martial, kun's middle line changes to kan as literary, kan's top line changes to xun as emolument, xun's middle line changes to gen as giant gate, gen's bottom line changes to li as broken army, li's middle line changing back is qian as assistant".

**Each of the eight stars occupies exactly one palace, never two in one** — the old table had a case of two stars sharing a palace, corrected.

**Upgraded to tier A on 2026-10-31 after *Yu Xia Ji* was added**: the song's eight lines placed gate by gate match the table generated by the flipping method in **all 56 cells** (the two methods corroborate each other); the nine stars' yin-yang and grades follow the original (life is most auspicious, heavenly doctor next, longevity next, severed life most inauspicious, five ghosts most inauspicious, six killings next inauspicious, mishap next inauspicious).

**What it produces**

Measured, 7 fields: `出生年`, `性别`, `命卦`, `命卦数`, `东西四命`, `八方` (eight wandering stars and eight-direction fortune), `三元命卦`, `口径`.

Three songs from *Yu Xia Ji* are also integrated — timing (nine stars' yearly portents), offspring, and chamber fortunes — **without converting to scores**.

**Disagreements**: fixed position (assistant) is given in the original as "left assistant next inauspicious, right undetermined", while convention has it as mildly auspicious; **both readings are preserved**. The three-yuan nine-palace method is presented alongside the great wandering year method.

**Tier**: **A**.

| Annual | Centre = ((9 − ((Gregorian year − 2000) mod 9)) mod 9) + 1 (2024 = 3 green, 2025 = 2 black) |
| Monthly | By **solar-term month** (first month = yin month from spring begins); zi/wu/mao/you years start the first month at 8 white, yin/shen/si/hai years at 2 black, chen/xu/chou/wei years at 5 yellow, stepping **backward** monthly into the centre, then flying forward |

### 4.9 Da Liu Ren (two functions)

#### 4.9.1 Liu Ren chart

**Basis**: *Liu Ren Da Quan*, chapter 1, "Worked Examples"; chapter 3, "Songs and Rhapsodies (Contemplating the Moon Sutra)"; chapters 7–10, "Collected Lessons".

**Algorithm**

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Monthly general | Changes at principal terms: rain water hai deng-ming / spring equinox xu he-kui / grain rain you cong-kui / grain full shen chuan-song / summer solstice wei xiao-ji / major heat wu sheng-guang / end of heat si tai-yi / autumn equinox chen tian-gang / frost descent mao tai-chong / minor snow yin gong-cao / winter solstice chou da-ji / major cold zi shen-hou |
| Heaven and earth plates | Heaven[x] = (x + M − T) mod 12 (M = monthly general branch index, T = divination hour branch index) |
| Day stem lodging | Jia-yin, yi-chen, bing-si, wu-si, ding-wei, ji-wei, geng-shen, xin-xu, ren-hai, gui-chou |
| Four lessons | Lodging → stem-above → above-above / day branch → branch-above → above-above |

The nine gates follow the original in this order: thief-and-overcoming → comparison/knowing-one → wading-harm → distant-overcoming → Pleiades → separate-responsibility → eight专 → repeated-chant → reverse-chant.

| Gate | Rule |
|---|---|
| Thief and overcoming | One above overcoming below = chief head; one below stealing from above = repeated trial; the overcome branch becomes the first transmission |
| Comparison / knowing one | Yang days take yang branches, yin days take yin branches; when all or none compare, enter wading-harm |
| Wading harm | Count depth of being overcome, take the deeper; when equal, the meng above-star is "seeing the mechanism", the zhong above-star "examining the subtle", and if still equal, "embellishing flaw" |
| Distant overcoming | Spirit distantly overcoming the day = mugwort arrow, **taken first**; day distantly overcoming the spirit = catapult, **taken after**; multiple lessons compared as in the comparison gate |
| Pleiades | Yang days take the spirit above you, yin days the spirit below you, as the first transmission; hard days take chen then day, soft days day then chen, as middle and last |
| Separate responsibility | Incomplete four lessons: hard days take the spirit above the stem's combined lodging, soft days the branch's forward triple harmony; middle and last both from the stem-above |
| Eight专 | Two lessons with stem and branch in the same position: yang days count three forward from the stem-above, yin days three backward from the branch's yin; middle and last from the stem-above; no distant overcoming |
| Repeated chant | With overcoming take it; without, hard days the stem-above and soft days the chen-above, with successive three punishments as middle and last; if the first transmission self-punishes, the middle reverses day and chen; if the middle self-punishes again, the last takes the clash |
| Reverse chant | With overcoming still use thief-and-overcoming / comparison / wading-harm, first and last identical while clashing the middle, not called "without reliance"; without overcoming the six days ding-chou, ji-chou, xin-chou, ding-wei, ji-wei, xin-wei are named well-rail / without reliance / without kin, taking the day branch's post horse as first, branch-above as middle, stem-above as last |

**Two places where the early implementation disagreed with the texts and were corrected**: (1) distant overcoming labelled "spirit overcoming the day" as catapult and "day overcoming the spirit" as mugwort arrow, with the priority reversed; (2) reverse chant with overcoming was wrongly called "without reliance", whereas "without reliance" refers specifically to the six days without overcoming.

The noble mnemonic uses the **official-school stem-combination derivation**: yang nobles counted forward from zi (jia-wei, yi-shen, bing-you, ding-hai, wu-chou, ji-zi, geng-chou, xin-yin, ren-mao, gui-si); yin nobles counted backward from shen (jia-chou, yi-zi, bing-hai, ding-you, wu-wei, ji-shen, geng-wei, xin-wu, ren-si, gui-mao). Per *Qin Ding Xie Ji Bian Fang Shu*, prefatory chapter quoting *Li Hai Ji*; the *Siku* abstract of *Liu Ren Da Quan* also corrects the popular "day chou night wei" mnemonic accordingly. Day = mao through shen; night = you through yin.

Heavenly general order: noble, flying serpent, vermilion bird, six harmony, hook-and-tangle, blue dragon, sky, white tiger, great constant, black warrior, great yin, queen of heaven; nobles placed forward when at hai/zi/chou/yin/mao/chen and backward at si/wu/wei/shen/you/xu.

**What it produces**

Measured, 33 top-level fields: `input`, `干支`, `月将`, `天地盘`, `四课`, `三传`, `发用`, `课体`, `昼夜`, `贵人`, `十二天将`, `旬首`, `旬空`, `驿马`, `月驿马`, `天马`, `三传遁干`, `连茹`, `日干支序`, `孤虚`, `本命`, `行年`, `命上`, `年上`, `应期`, `口径`, `杂课`, `将性`, `将态`, `将象`, `年命判`.

**Tier**: **A**. Noted: heavenly and monthly virtue are not implemented because the Liu Ren plate has no four-meng positions (the virtue-celebration lesson takes only the two stem-branch virtues).

#### 4.9.2 Bi Fa analysis

**Basis**: *Liu Ren Da Quan*, chapter 11, "Bi Fa Fu (upper)", items 1–70; chapter 12, "Bi Fa Fu (lower)", items 71–100; chapter 1, "Explanation of the Twelve Generals".

**Algorithm**

**95 of the 100 items** are machine-decidable. Three judgements were corrected against worked examples in the original:

| Item | Original worked example | Old implementation's error |
|---|---|---|
| 17/18/73 "all void" | "Ren-zi day, stem-above zi, three transmissions yin, mao, chen, all void" — a xun has only two void branches, so three transmissions all falling in the same xun's void is structurally impossible; "all void" therefore means **the first and middle transmissions fall in this xun's void** | Judged as "all three void", which scanned zero occurrences across the whole period |

## 5. Highlights

All seven are reproducible and verifiable. Each gives the name, the value, the implementation, the difference from common approaches, and the supporting evidence.

### Highlight 1: The calendar is recomputed bottom-up, no almanac table

- **Name**: Real-time new-moon computation from astronomical series.
- **Value**: Solves "always wrong on boundary days". Leap-month attribution and month change both fall on new-moon and solar-term instants, exactly where tables are weakest — and the error propagates to every downstream module.
- **Implementation**: Gregorian → Julian Day → solar longitude (truncated VSOP87 + nutation / aberration / ΔT) → true new moon (Shou Xing precision table `SHUO_KB` before 1985, series after) → month from the new-moon day → leap month between two winter solstices when a month has no principal term.
- **Differentiation**: Common open-source implementations embed an almanac table (or depend on a third-party package), fail outside its coverage, and cannot answer "why is this the first day of a leap month". Here every intermediate quantity can be printed.
- **Evidence**: Solar terms accurate to the second — 3,576 terms from 1901 to 2049 compared against a high-precision reference: mean deviation 0 s, p99 = 1 s, maximum 60 s, all dates identical. Leap months all correct: 2004 and 2023 leap second month, 2020 leap fourth month, 1984 leap tenth month, 1903 leap fifth month, plus 1914 new-moon days from an older era; 1985 correctly has no leap month (first day of the first month = 20 February).

### Highlight 2: All algorithms self-implemented, zero embedded third-party code

- **Name**: Zero embedded dependencies in the core.
- **Value**: Solves "I don't know where this result comes from". An embedded third-party distribution means an area of the core that cannot be audited or corrected against source texts.
- **Implementation**: Before the 5.0.0 rebuild the core embedded third-party calendar and Zi Wei packages; the rebuild **removed all of them**. Lunar calendar, sexagenary, solar terms, Zi Wei star placement, Qi Men, Liu Ren, Liu Yao, Mei Hua and flying stars are all independently implemented across 61 files and 12,518 lines.

## 6. Design Details and Small Decisions

### 5.1 Input normalisation: nine formats converge on one chart

`src/core/features/normalizer.js` handles the following so that `1990年6月15日`, `1990-6-15`, `1990.6.15`, `1990/6/15`, `15/6/1990`, `19900615`, a zero-width-contaminated string, full-width digits and Chinese numerals all yield the same chart:

| Item | Approach | Why it must be handled |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-width and BOM | Stripped outright | Dates copied from web pages often carry invisible zero-width characters that break parsing |
| Full-width and fancy digits | Full-width converted to half-width; superscripts and subscripts normalised | Full-width digits are extremely common with Chinese input methods |
| English months | `Jan` / `January` etc. converted to numbers | Users copy from English material |
| Chinese numerals | Converted to Arabic, **but 60-plus function words are placeholder-protected** | The digits inside 「排八字」or「三奇」are function words; converting them breaks matching |
| Separators | `年/月/日`, `-`, `.`, `/`, space unified | Too many formats to branch on individually |
| Digit-run splitting | Split by 14/12/8-digit date-time, 6-digit YYMMDD, reversed D-M-Y, date followed by HHMM, isolated MMDD; **9–11 digits treated as an ID number and never split** | 19900615 is a date; 440101199006151234 is an ID. Length is the only reliable discriminator |

### 5.2 Boundaries between defaults and refusals

- A missing double-hour defaults to 14:00: fate functions need four pillars, and defaulting to the eighth double-hour beats failing, since most users do not know their birth hour.
- Fate functions without birth data return one of four refusal codes (`need_birth` / `invalid_lunar` / `invalid_pillar` / `invalid_solar`) and prompt in place; nothing is guessed.
- Query functions (today's almanac, lunar conversion) are not gated on birth data: checking today does not require knowing your own birthday.

### 5.3 Three result-page branches

| Branch | Trigger | Layout |
|---|---|---|
| Single algorithm | One sentence matches one function | Single column |
| Multiple matches | Several functions matched (e.g. "BaZi ZiWei") | Each section `{title, text}` side by side |
| Full chart set | Birth data only, no function name | Seven grouped categories |

Uniform two-column "label: value" layout, with long values wrapping automatically.

### 5.4 Three storage promises

1. **Reads never throw**: any storage read failure returns a default rather than letting an exception reach the interface.
2. **Missing keys get defaults**: never `undefined`.
3. **Only this project's prefix is cleared** (`xael_`): clearing cache never deletes another app's data on the same origin.

1 MB cap; on overflow the oldest entries are evicted first, never failing silently.

### 5.5 Error guard

Only `window`'s `error` and `unhandledrejection` are observed, under three rules:

- **Never swallow**: logged and still visible in the console;
- **Never leak stack traces**: reports contain no file paths or call stacks;
- **Rate-limited**: the same error is reported once per 60 seconds, so a loop cannot flood.

### 5.6 Three-tier offline fallback

Service Worker request order: network → cache → offline page. `404.html` makes zero external requests — an offline page that pulls from a CDN cannot itself open while offline.

### 5.7 Visual and unit discipline

- Light/dark themes cross-fade over 320 ms, manual preference overriding system, with `wx.setBackgroundColor` synced on the mini program;
- Four responsive bands: <360 px full width, 360–767 design width, 768–1023 centred at 640, ≥1024 centred at 720, font sizes constant;
- Units are always px / rem, **never rpx** (conditional compilation for the uni-app shell is documented in `docs/DEPLOY.md`);
- Splash holds 1.2 s then a 400 ms scale hand-off; press feedback 120 ms; dialogs play first then dismiss;
- Image export mirrors the result layout at 2×, auto-paginating beyond 4096 pt with "(continued)" and "page i of n" markers.

### 8.3 MCP (stdio)

`mcp/server.js` exposes three tools: `xael_analyze`, `xael_list_features` and `xael_schema`. JSON-RPC 2.0 framed by line, consumed as a subprocess by clients such as Claude Desktop and Cursor.

### 8.4 Skill card and expert pack

| Form | Entry | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Skill | `skills/xael/SKILL.md` | AI skill card: when to call, how to format input, how to handle the seven `status` values |
| Expert pack | `experts/xael/5.0.0/` | Expert identity card `agents/xael-charting-expert.md` (chart + plain-language reading + textual basis as a three-part method) |

**Contract**: every form shares one layer, `bin/xael-api.js` (`xael/analysis@1`), so command line and MCP output are necessarily identical.

The seven `status` values: `ok` / `need_birth` / `invalid_lunar` / `invalid_pillar` / `invalid_solar` / `refused` / `rejected`; on multiple matches, `sections` provides `{title, text}` per section. Smoke coverage is in `tests/ai-entry.test.js` (14 assertions).

## 10. Configuration

There is no runtime configuration file. Adjustable items fall into two classes.

### 9.1 Build-time switches (environment variables)

| Variable | Effect | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `XAEL_BUILD_TARGET` | Build target (`web` / `mp` / `uni`) | Determined by script argument |
| `XAEL_STRICT` | Strict mode: abort the build on any failed assertion | On |

### 9.2 Convention tables (how to switch schools)

School choices are collected in `docs/CALIBER-NOTES.md` and the corresponding module constant tables. Switching schools means changing one table, not the logic:

| Convention | Current value | How to switch |
|---|---|---|
| Zi Wei leap-month attribution | Fifteenth counted in the current month | Change the leap-month constant in `purple-star/chart.js` |
| Four transformations | Zhongzhou mainstream | Change the ten-stem transformation table in `purple-star/transformations.js` |
| Qi Men ju determination | *Chai-bu* | Change the ju function in `mystic-gates/hour-chart.js` |
| Liu Ren day/night noble | Mao through shen | Change the day/night boundary in `six-ren/heavenly-generals.js` |
| Quarter system | Ten quarters per double-hour (12 min each) | Change the quarter constant in `mystic-gates/quarter-chart.js` |

Changing a convention requires updating `docs/CALIBER-NOTES.md`, and `npm run check` verifies consistency.

## 16. Known Limitations

1. **One school chosen where disagreement is wide**: quarter charts, Bao Jian day charts, year and month charts and flying plates have strongly divergent schools; one is chosen and stated, with no guarantee of matching commercial software.
2. **95 of the 100 Bi Fa items are decidable**: the other 5 (93/97/98/99/100) are methodology for reading a lesson with no independent plate criterion, so they are catalogued but not judged; item 56 requires the querent's life branch to be supplied.
3. **Qi Men patterns limited to ten-stem interactions**: undetected patterns are simply not generated — omission over invention.
4. **Holiday data has a cutoff**: current through the 2026 State Council announcement; later years require a source update.
5. **Second-level differences from the official astronomical almanac**: irrelevant to divination use, but unsuitable for astronomical observation.
6. **Unverified textual items**: Tai Ji noble, golden chariot and red beauty have no textual source and are marked "unverified" rather than dressed up as sourced.

## 19. Contributing

### 18.1 Two rules

1. **A convention change must update `docs/CALIBER-NOTES.md`.** Changing the table without the document creates a second source of truth.
2. **Calendar boundary behaviour must have test assertions.** Leap months, solar-term transitions and year boundaries are where errors concentrate; changes without assertions are not merged.

### 18.2 Pre-submit checks

```bash
npm test            # 1,472 assertions
npm run check       # twelve pre-release self-checks
npm run check:cites # citation audit
npm run audit:invariants
npm run check:refs
npm run check:readings
```

### 18.3 Filing an issue

- **Convention challenge**: include the textual basis (book, chapter, original wording). "This is wrong" without a source is not accepted.
- **Bug report**: include reproducible input and expected output. For calendar issues, give the exact date and the expected lunar date or solar term.
- **Feature request**: state which school it belongs to and which text it rests on.
